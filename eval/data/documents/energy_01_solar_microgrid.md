# Solar Microgrids

A solar microgrid combines local generation, storage, and loads that can operate with or without a larger utility grid. Solar panels produce variable power, so batteries or another source help balance demand when sunlight changes.

A controller decides when to use solar generation, charge batteries, draw from the grid, or reduce flexible loads. The best control policy depends on electricity prices, weather forecasts, battery limits, and reliability requirements.

Microgrids can improve resilience for schools, clinics, and remote communities. Their design should include protection equipment, safe islanding behavior, maintenance plans, and a clear method for reconnecting to the main grid.

The electrical design must account for peak load, inverter capacity, battery state of charge, and the difference between critical and flexible loads. A clinic may reserve stored energy for refrigeration and medical equipment while temporarily reducing lighting or air-conditioning. Load prioritization should be agreed before an outage rather than improvised during one.

Performance should be measured with more than the number of installed panels. Useful measures include renewable energy fraction, unmet critical load, battery cycling, curtailment, fuel use, and time spent operating independently. Weather uncertainty and equipment maintenance should appear in the simulation so the design is not evaluated only under ideal sunlight.
