# Supporting Pollinators

Pollinator-friendly gardens provide flowers, shelter, water, and protection from unnecessary pesticides. A mix of plant shapes and bloom times can support bees, butterflies, and other insects across a longer season.

Native plants are often well adapted to local weather and local insect relationships, but a garden can use both native and well-behaved non-native species. Avoiding invasive plants matters more than following a single universal plant list.

Pesticides should not be the first response to minor damage. Identify the pest, check whether natural predators are present, and use the least harmful effective treatment when intervention is necessary.

Flower color alone does not determine whether a plant supports pollinators. Flower shape, nectar availability, bloom period, and access for different insect body sizes all matter. Including early, middle, and late-season flowers can prevent a garden from providing one short burst of food followed by a long gap.

Shelter may include undisturbed soil, leaf litter, hollow stems, and dense vegetation. Water should be shallow and kept clean so insects can land without drowning. A small garden can contribute habitat when several balconies, yards, or community plots use complementary plants instead of treating each space as an isolated display.
