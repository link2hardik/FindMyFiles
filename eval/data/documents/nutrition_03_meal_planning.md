# Simple Meal Planning

Meal planning reduces last-minute decisions by choosing a few meals and preparing overlapping ingredients. A basic plate can include vegetables or fruit, a protein source, a grain or starchy food, and a source of unsaturated fat.

A weekly plan does not need seven unique recipes. Leftover grains, roasted vegetables, beans, eggs, or cooked chicken can be reused in bowls, wraps, soups, and salads. Keeping a short list of fast meals makes the plan more realistic.

Budget, culture, allergies, schedule, and cooking equipment all influence a workable plan. The best plan is one that can be repeated without creating excessive waste or effort.

Planning can start with a small template rather than a rigid menu. Choose two proteins, two grains or starchy foods, several vegetables, and a few flexible flavor ingredients. A batch of cooked beans can become a soup one day and a filling for wraps the next. Frozen and canned foods can be practical options when fresh ingredients are expensive or unavailable.

Food safety belongs in the plan. Cooked leftovers should be cooled and stored promptly, labeled with a date, and reheated safely. Portioning some meals before eating can help reduce waste, while keeping a short emergency meal list makes it less likely that a busy day will eliminate the whole plan.
